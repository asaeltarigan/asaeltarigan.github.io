Unemployment in Indonesia remains a pressing issue, with 7.86 million individuals recorded as unemployed in 2023, including 5.32 percent in the open unemployment category. Many fresh graduates fail at the interview stage due to low confidence and limited preparation. Existing training tools provide general practice but lack integrated, data-driven feedback on both verbal and non-verbal performance. This study introduces InterQ, a web-based platform that combines facial expression analysis using ResEmoteNet and Haar Cascade with automated answer grading through Whisper transcription and E5-small embeddings. The models were validated on FER-2013 and synthetic Q&A datasets, achieving average F1-scores of 0.747 and 0.706. A usability study with 50 participants using a validated questionnaire showed that 80.4 percent felt more confident and better prepared for real interviews. These findings highlight InterQ’s novelty as a multi-modal AI simulator and its potential to address interview challenges faced by Indonesian job seekers.
