The proliferation of generative models has enabled increasingly realistic deepfake content, posing serious risks to digital trust and public information integrity. While convolutional neural networks such as XceptionNet achieve near-perfect accuracy on intra-dataset deepfake benchmarks, their performance typically deteriorates on samples drawn from unseen distributions, a phenomenon known as the generalization gap. This study proposes a Multi-Source Fine-Tuning (MSFT) pipeline designed to mitigate dataset-specific overfitting in deepfake forensics. An ImageNet-pretrained XceptionNet backbone is adapted across two distinct distributions, namely a GAN-based facial image dataset of 140k real and fake faces and a combined FaceForensics++ and Celeb-DF video-frame distribution, through a two-phase training regimen: feature extraction with a frozen backbone, followed by end-to-end fine-tuning at an ultra-low learning rate to mitigate catastrophic forgetting. The classification head replaces the original ImageNet layers with Global Average Pooling, two regularized dense layers, and a sigmoid output. Evaluation on a held-out subset of 2,400 samples, using a strict authenticity confidence threshold of 0.70, yielded an overall accuracy of 87%, a recall of 96% on manipulated media, and only 43 false positives, demonstrating the pipeline's suitability as a precision-oriented forensic filter. Qualitative analysis identifies heavy JPEG compression, low-light environments, and multi-face frames as the principal remaining failure modes.
