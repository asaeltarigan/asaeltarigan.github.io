Handwritten signatures are still one of the most widely accepted ways to authenticate financial, legal, and administrative documents. Checking them by hand, however, is slow, subjective, and often unreliable once a forgery has been practiced. This paper presents a writer-independent signature verification system built around a deliberately simple idea: rather than retraining a deep network end to end, we freeze a pretrained EfficientNet-B0 backbone and fine-tune only its embedding head with batch-hard triplet loss. The head was trained on genuine signatures from 221 writers pooled from CEDAR, GPDS, and a locally collected dataset. Each signature is mapped to a 512-dimensional L2-normalized embedding, and pairs are compared by cosine similarity. Evaluation used 33 writers held out entirely from training, and we report random-impostor and skilled-forgery performance separately, a distinction that much of the literature omits. Fine-tuning raised the random-impostor Area Under the Curve (AUC) from 0.953 to 0.979, with the Equal Error Rate falling from 10.71% to 7.07%, and raised the skilled-forgery AUC from 0.827 to 0.868, with the Equal Error Rate falling from 24.85% to 21.01%. Verification accuracy reached 90.20% against random impostors and 79.09% against skilled forgeries. The trained model is deployed in a lightweight web application that works in two modes: offline verification of uploaded or scanned images, and online verification of signatures drawn in real time on a digital whiteboard, both handled through the same image-based pipeline. The whole procedure runs in minutes on a CPU, and every reported number comes from a single reproducible evaluation script. Accepted for presentation at ICoDiT 2026 (Paper ID 155).
