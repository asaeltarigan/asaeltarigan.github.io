Theoretical and probabilistic foundations of deep learning, based on Bishop and Bishop's *Deep Learning: Foundations and Concepts*. The course emphasizes the mathematical principles, probabilistic models, and emerging architectures underlying modern AI systems, and serves as the bridge between Machine Learning (Semester 2) and applied NLP and Computer Vision (Semester 4).

## Weekly Sessions (13 weeks)

| Week | Topic | Focus |
|------|-------|-------|
| 1 | The Deep Learning Revolution | Historical evolution, drivers, ethical considerations |
| 2 | Probabilistic Foundations | Probability theory, Bayes' theorem, probabilistic modeling |
| 3 | Standard Distributions | Gaussian, Bernoulli, Categorical, Softmax |
| 4 | Single-layer Networks: Regression | Linear regression, loss functions, model fitting |
| 5 | Single-layer Networks: Classification | Logistic regression, decision boundaries, metrics |
| 6 | Deep Neural Networks | Multilayer perceptrons, activation functions, depth and capacity |
| 7 | Optimization and Backpropagation | Gradient descent, learning rates, backpropagation mathematics |
| 8 | Regularization Techniques | Dropout, early stopping, L1/L2 regularization, overfitting |
| 9 | Convolutional Neural Networks | Feature maps, pooling, hierarchical feature learning |
| 10 | Transformers and Attention | Self-attention, positional encoding, encoder-decoder structures |
| 11 | Graph Neural Networks | Message passing, node embeddings, graph applications |
| 12 | Generative Models and Latent Variables | GANs, autoencoders, normalizing flows, variational principles |
| 13 | Emerging Models and Future Directions | Diffusion models, multimodal learning |

## Labs (weeks 4, 5, 6, 9, 11, 12)

Hands-on sessions on single-layer networks, MLP playground, CNN filters and feature maps, GNN node classification, and generative models with autoencoders.
